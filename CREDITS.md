### What "enhanced" means here

The two photographs arrived at 1470 and 1680 pixels wide, below the 2200 the
card asks for on a large screen. They were enlarged with a Lanczos filter and
sharpened — arithmetic on the pixels that are there. They were **not** passed
through a generative model. An image model asked to "enhance" a photograph
redraws it: it would invent architectural detail the building does not have
and new faces for the people in the foyer, and the result would no longer be
a photograph of anywhere. The recipe is in the commit that added the files and
takes a few seconds to re-run if better originals arrive.

The admin page says in so many words that only a picture the organiser holds
the rights to may be put there — their own, their photographer's, or one with a
licence that permits it.

## Project photographs

The images on site 2's project cards are the client's own: they came embedded in
the `.pptx` sheets the organiser supplied, and `scripts/build-project-photos.mjs`
pulls them out, matched to the slide they belong to. The Madaniyat Hotel's
renders come from its concept PDF in the same way.

Nothing is sourced from anywhere else. Two classes of image are **deliberately
dropped**:

- **Screenshots of online maps.** Several sheets illustrate their location with a
  grab of a mapping service. Those are not the client's to republish, so they are
  detected and discarded — a map tile has a large area of flat near-neutral paper
  and almost no texture, which a photograph does not.
- **Template furniture.** The forum lockup, the masthead rule and the section
  icons repeat on every slide of a deck, so anything appearing on more than two
  slides is dropped.

Automatic tests cannot tell a render of this project from a stock photograph of
somebody else's, so **every lead image was looked at**. Fourteen were rejected by
hand and listed in the script with the reason — satellite grabs with a plot drawn
on them, a collage of European outlet villages, a photograph of Disneyland Paris,
a stock jet-ski, a map pin graphic. Six projects are left without a picture
because their sheets contain nothing else; better no picture than somebody
else's theme park.

What remains includes site plans and concept boards the client assembled
themselves. Those are published as supplied; if any turns out not to be the
client's own, it should be added to that list.

## Geographic data

**Natural Earth** — 110m physical land (coastlines), v5.1.2.

- Licence: **public domain.** "No permission is needed to use Natural Earth.
  Crediting the authors is unnecessary." It is credited here anyway.
- Source URL: `https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector` (tag `v5.1.2`)

Processing (`scripts/build-globe.mjs`): exterior rings only — interior rings
at this scale are lakes — simplified with Douglas–Peucker at 0.55° and rings
under 2.5° across dropped, because the globe is about five hundred pixels
across and a ring smaller than a pixel is a speck that still costs a draw.
5 091 positions → 1 350. It is used as lines on the title card's globe and
nowhere else; nothing is measured off it and no boundary is asserted by it.

**geoBoundaries** — Open Administrative Boundaries, gbOpen release.

- Dataset: Uzbekistan ADM1 (2017), 14 first-order administrative divisions
- Licence: **Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0**
- Source URL: `https://github.com/wmgeolab/geoBoundaries` (release `9469f09`)
- Upstream source: `wambachers-osm.website/boundaries/`
- Citation: Runfola, D. et al. (2020) *geoBoundaries: A global database of political
  administrative boundaries.* PLoS ONE 15(4): e0231866.

Processing (`scripts/build-geo.mjs`): properties reduced to the region `id` used
in `content.json`, rings simplified with Douglas–Peucker and rounded to four
decimals, and rewound to the orientation d3-geo expects. 6 206 positions → 2 733;
170 KB → 49 KB. The ODbL requires that this derived database stay under the same
licence, which it does; the attribution appears in the site 3 footer.

Site 2 draws the same boundaries in chapter IV. It does not ship the GeoJSON:
`scripts/build-region-map.mjs` projects it once (Albers equal-area conic,
standard parallels 38.5°/44.5°, central meridian 66°E) into flat SVG paths in a
1000 × 560 box, with a label point per region found by pole-of-inaccessibility
search. 49 KB → 33 KB, and no projection library in the browser. The same ODbL
terms apply to that derived file. The credit used to be printed under the map
as well as in the footer; the line under the map came off at the client's
request, so the footer's is now the only one and it names the region map as
well as the globe.

The title card draws the national outline from the same release — ADM0 rather
than ADM1, the same pinned commit — simplified to 277 positions and projected
onto the sphere, with a flattened copy for the no-WebGL still. Same ODbL terms,
same attribution.

## Ornament

The girih field, the panjara, the rosette and the corner mark in site 2 are
**generated by this repository** — `scripts/build-ornament.mjs` — and are
nobody else's work. Each is a geometric construction rather than a drawing:
the field and the rosette's heart are regular {8/3} octagrams, the corner mark
is a lozenge, the rosette's petals are eight compass arcs, and the panjara is
six-pointed star rings on a triangular lattice, their points set on the
lattice directions so the ties between them close the screen. The pointed arch
that a project's photograph, a direction's icon and an empty picture slot are
seen through is one path defined in the site's own markup. There is nothing
here to licence and nobody to credit.

The motifs belong to the architecture the page is about — the revetments of the
Registan and Shah-i-Zinda, and the embroidery and carved ganch of an interior.
They are used as decoration only. **No State symbol is reproduced, stylised or
stood in for.** The State Emblem appears once, inside the organiser's own
lockup, where it is placed unaltered and is not restyled by any of this.

## Cloth — generated, and currently unused

`scripts/build-ikat.mjs` generates Uzbek atlas and adras — warp ikat, where
the silk is tied and dyed before it is woven, so every colour boundary has
bled along the thread and carries a fine toothed edge. The generator draws
that feathering first and the motifs second — the qalampir or bodom (the
pepper, or almond), the medallion, the sham (candle) and the jiyak chevron
braid that finishes a chapan's edge — each cut into slices a few units tall
and pushed sideways on a repeating comb. The seed is fixed, so the same cloth
is produced on every build. It is this repository's own work; no photograph of
cloth is used and none is needed.

**No page currently shows it.** The cloth ran along the seams and down the
stage edges of site 2 for a few days and was taken out: a woven motif is read
by its shape, a shape needs area, and a 300x64 tile squeezed into a fourteen
pixel band across fourteen hundred arrives as a row of coloured specks. The
generator and the tiles are kept because the cloth is not wrong — it is wrong
at thread scale — and `src/styles/ikat.css` is still built, simply not
imported. This entry stays so that nothing turns up later with no provenance.

The colours are those a dyer uses and, not by accident, close to the national
ones. **The flag is used as a source of colour and nothing else.** It is not
drawn, not stretched across anything and not woven into a pattern; the one
place the national colours appear as themselves is a three-band rule beneath
the name of the State body organising the forum, and that rule carries neither
the crescent nor the stars. There is nothing here to licence and nobody to
credit.

### Montserrat, and how it was identified

The forum artwork the organiser supplied carries three lines of type —
*Tourism / Investment / Forum 2026* — with every glyph already converted to
outlines, so no file in `logo/` names the typeface it was set in. The forum's
full name has two more words in front of it, and setting those in the site's
body face put two typefaces inside one piece of lettering.

The face was established by measurement, not by eye. Every glyph of all three
lines was cut out of the artwork as a connected run of ink, normalised, and
scored against forty candidate families rendered at the same cap height:

| Line | Closest match | Overlap | Runner-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tourism | Montserrat Regular | 97.2% | Inter Light, 91.9% |
| Investment | Montserrat SemiBold | 98.9% | Figtree SemiBold, 91.1% |
| Forum 2026 | Montserrat Regular | 97.3% | Red Hat Display Regular, 87.6% |

Montserrat's own metrics then confirmed it: a cap height of 700/1000 puts the
artwork at 60.04 units, at which Montserrat sets "Forum 2026" within half a
percent of the width it actually occupies.

`brand/fonts/Montserrat-Regular.ttf` is the latin subset served by Google
Fonts, kept here so `npm run logos` needs no network. `brand/fonts/OFL.txt` is
the licence it travels under. The font is **not** shipped to the browser:
`scripts/lib/forum-title.mjs` converts the two lines to outlines at build time
— one path per glyph, as the supplied artwork is drawn — and they become part
of the lockup's vector artwork, which is what the OFL's terms are written to
allow.

Note on site 3: IBM Plex places the Uzbek apostrophes `ʻ` (U+02BB) and `ʼ`
(U+02BC) on a 0.6em advance, which splits every word containing one. A
`@font-face` claiming only those two codepoints borrows them from a system UI
font; see the comment at the top of `site-3-explorer/src/styles/app.css`.

## Client source material

The Tourism Investment Forum content on site 2 comes from documents supplied by
the organiser for this project: the forum concept, the preliminary programme, and
three batches of investment project sheets (`onepager .zip`, `Лойиҳалар new.zip`,
`Лойиҳалар.zip`), plus the corrected sheets sent since, which arrive in a
dated folder of their own (`brand/loyhainfo/<date>/`). They are read by
`scripts/build-forum-data.mjs` and are **not redistributed** — the originals stay
out of the repository and only the parsed facts are published.

The sheets arrive in three generations of the same template with different field
lists, and the same project appears in more than one batch. The newest sheet for
a project wins — a dated folder outranks every archive — and every sheet it was
found in is recorded on the record, so a figure can be traced back.

### The City of Tashkent's proposal deck

One document in the batch of 21 October 2026 is not a one-pager deck:
*Истиқболли лойиҳа таклифлари* is nine proposals from the city for raising
visitor numbers, in three different layouts and written in Uzbek Cyrillic, which
is not one of the three scripts this site publishes in. It could not be parsed
and it could not be published as it stood, so it was read and translated by hand
into `scripts/data/tashkent-proposals.mjs`, the way the forum's own framing was
transcribed from the two Russian concept documents.

**No figure in it was retyped from memory.** Every number on those nine records
carries the exact run of text it was read from, and the build checks each one
against the slide it names before writing anything — 41 of them. A revised deck,
or a figure that was never on the slide, stops the build.

The project sheets contain an `INVESTMENT CONTACT` block with an initiator's name
and personal mobile number, and the raw Tashkent-region tables add taxpayer
identification numbers and bank details. **None of it is carried into
`forum-2026.json` or onto the page** — the contact block is parsed in order to be
discarded.
